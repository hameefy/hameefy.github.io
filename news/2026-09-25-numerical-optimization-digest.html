Numerical Optimization Digest · Issue 7

Randomized preconditioning, reversible Markov chains, and nonconvex splitting

Published 25 September 2026 · Curated by Hassan Mohammad

Coverage: first-online publications and first-posted preprints from 18–24 September 2026. Two peer-reviewed Springer Nature articles and one arXiv preprint met this issue’s methodological threshold.

Peer-reviewed publications

Two articles with substantial numerical-method contributions. Publisher publication dates determine inclusion in this section.

1. Robust, randomized preconditioning for kernel ridge regression

Mateo Díaz, Ethan N. Epperly, Zachary Frangella, Joel A. Tropp and Robert J. Webber · Advances in Computational Mathematics · Springer Nature · First online 18 September 2026 · DOI: 10.1007/s10444-026-10360-1 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Conjugate-gradient methods · optimization in machine learning · numerical linear algebra · kernel ridge regression

Why it matters
Large kernel ridge regression problems are limited by the conditioning and cost of their linear systems. The authors analyze two randomized preconditioners for complementary full-data and restricted regimes.

Main result claims

  • Full KRR: With sufficiently rapid eigenvalue decay of the kernel matrix, RPCholesky-preconditioned conjugate gradients attain fixed accuracy in O(N2) arithmetic operations.
  • Restricted KRR: With k ≪ N centers, KRILL requires O((N + k2)k log k) operations without an eigenvalue-decay assumption.
  • Experiments: Tests on benchmark and scientific datasets report robust behavior against existing preconditioners.
  • Qualification: The full-KRR complexity guarantee assumes sufficiently rapid spectral decay; KRILL’s guarantee addresses the restricted problem.

Read first
The RPCholesky and KRILL complexity analysis, the numerical experiments, and Appendix 1’s blocked RPCholesky pseudocode.

2. Kemeny’s constant minimization for reversible Markov chains via structure-preserving perturbations

Fabio Durastante, Miryam Gnazzo and Beatrice Meini · BIT Numerical Mathematics · Springer Nature · First online 23 September 2026 · DOI: 10.1007/s10543-026-01152-6 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Riemannian optimization · conjugate-gradient methods · constrained optimization · numerical linear algebra

Why it matters
The authors optimize a measure of a Markov chain’s mixing efficiency while preserving reversibility, a fixed stationary distribution, and a prescribed sparsity pattern.

Main result claims

  • Formulation: Restricting the chain to reversible transition matrices makes the Kemeny-constant minimization problem convex over the structured feasible set.
  • Methods: The paper develops interior-point and Riemannian optimization approaches, including first-order geometry on the manifold of structured stochastic matrices.
  • Experiments: Numerical examples compare the methods and show accurate preservation of reversibility and stationarity by the Riemannian approach, with competitive reductions in Kemeny’s constant.
  • Qualification: The framework assumes reversibility and a prescribed perturbation pattern; it does not solve the unrestricted Markov-chain problem.

Read first
The feasible-manifold and Riemannian-gradient construction, then the numerical comparisons with the interior-point and SDP formulations.

arXiv preprints

One independently selected preprint with a new whole-sequence convergence argument.

1. Whole-Sequence Convergence of Variable-Smoothing Full-Splitting Methods via a Lifted Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz Framework

Min Tao · arXiv:2609.28306 · First posted 23 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · proximal and gradient methods · global convergence · convergence analysis

Why it matters
Variable smoothing changes the model at every iteration, frustrating a direct fixed-objective convergence argument. The author constructs fixed lifted potentials to control the effects of that change.

Main result claims

  • Convergence: Under a Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz assumption on a fixed lifted potential and weighted summability of smoothing-change residuals, a scaled finite-length argument establishes convergence of the entire primal sequence to an exact limiting lifted stationary point.
  • Schedules: For power schedules γk = (k + k0)−β, with 1/2 < β ≤ 1, the required summability holds when the lift exponent is sufficiently large.
  • Extension: A corollary covers variable-smoothing full-splitting projected-gradient methods for nonconvex nonsmooth composite optimization without a full-row-rank assumption on the linear operators.
  • Qualification: The KL condition and residual-summability hypothesis are substantive assumptions; this preprint has not been peer reviewed in this digest’s publisher category.

Read first
The fixed lifted-potential construction, sufficient-decrease and relative-error estimates, and the finite-length argument; then the power-schedule corollary.
