Certified quasi-Newton curvature, tensor-free Newton complexity, and monotone compositions

Peer-reviewed publications

Two ranked publisher articles; the first-online date, rather than journal issue date, determines eligibility.

1. AGDA+: Proximal Alternating Gradient Descent Ascent Method with a Nonmonotone Adaptive Step-Size Search for Nonconvex Minimax Problems

Xuan Zhang, Qiushui Xu and Necdet Serhat Aybat · SIAM Journal on Optimization · SIAM · First online 29 October 2026 · DOI 10.1137/24M1692277 · Publisher record ↗

Priority topics
Line search · nonconvex optimization · primal–dual methods

Why it matters
An adaptive step-size method for composite nonconvex–strongly concave minimax problems.

Main result claims

  • The authors choose nonmonotone primal and dual step sizes through backtracking, without prior knowledge of the global smoothness constant or strong-concavity modulus.
  • For a jointly smooth coupling, strongly concave maximization variable, and closed convex regularizers, they establish O(ε⁻²) stationarity iteration complexity; each backtracking iteration uses O(1) gradient calls.
  • Numerical experiments support robustness and efficiency. These empirical claims are not a universal runtime guarantee, and strong concavity excludes general nonconvex–nonconcave games.

Read first
Start with the assumptions and AGDA+ step-size search, then the stationarity complexity theorem. The publisher abstract and history verify the summary and first-online date.

2. A cubic regularization method for multiobjective optimization

Douglas S. Gonçalves, Max L. N. Gonçalves and Jefferson G. Melo · Computational Optimization and Applications · Springer Nature · First online 23 October 2026 · DOI 10.1007/s10589-026-00847-4 · Publisher record ↗

Priority topics
Unconstrained optimization · cubic regularization · convergence rates

Why it matters
Extends cubic models to multiple objectives with implementable derivative-error controls.

Main result claims

  • The authors jointly adjust regularization and derivative accuracy through nonmonotone acceptance, allowing approximate gradients and Hessians.
  • With globally Lipschitz component Hessians, a lower-bounded component, and the prescribed inexactness conditions, they obtain O(Cε⁻³ᐟ²) iterations to an approximate Pareto critical point. This counts iterations, not all finite-difference evaluations.
  • Under additional local convexity conditions, exact gradients support superlinear convergence and exact first and second derivatives support quadratic convergence. Experiments compare against a safeguarded multiobjective Newton method; they do not establish general Pareto-front recovery.

Read first
Sections 3.1–3.3 for complexity, local assumptions and finite-difference costs; Section 4 for experiments.

arXiv preprints

Four ranked preprints. These results have not been established here as peer-reviewed publications; claims remain attributable to their authors.

1. Improved Gradient Complexity for Quasi-Newton Proximal Extragradient Methods via Certified Curvature Reuse

Aryan Mokhtari and Francisco Patitucci · arXiv:2610.40608 · Initial arXiv submission 29 October 2026 (UTC) · Primary record ↗ · Paper v1 ↗

Priority topics
Quasi-Newton · gradient complexity · nonconvex optimization

Why it matters
Replaces online matrix-learning regret control with a certificate for reused curvature.

Main result claims

  • One finite-difference probe refreshes a group of Hessian columns; a path-length certificate controls stale columns. The authors use at most three gradient queries per iteration, plus a regularized solve or trust-region step.
  • For convex problems they prove O(min{k⁻², χk⁻³}) objective-error decay. Strongly convex analysis gives an accelerated transient followed by a superlinear upper envelope, with other problem parameters fixed.
  • The nonconvex construction gives O(min{χ¹ᐟ³ε⁻³ᐟ², ε⁻⁷ᐟ⁴}) gradient queries. Here χ is a supplied sparsity-coloring dimension, equal to d without structure.
  • The bounds require exact gradient queries and stated smoothness, parameter and solve conditions. Known smoothness bounds and an initial-distance bound enter the convex setup; low oracle counts do not remove matrix-storage or linear-algebra costs.

Read first
Assumption 2.1, Theorem 3.5, Theorem 4.2 and Corollary 5.12; then Section 6 on inexact solves.

2. Proximal Point Newton Methods for Nonconvex Optimization

Chang He and Shuzhong Zhang · arXiv:2610.36256 · Initial arXiv submission 26 October 2026 (UTC) · Primary record ↗ · Paper v1 ↗

Priority topics
Newton–CG · unconstrained optimization · second-order stationarity

Why it matters
Obtains higher-order complexity without explicit derivative tensors.

Main result claims

  • For a lower-bounded objective with Lipschitz Hessian, alternating Newton and negative-curvature steps reaches an (εg, εH) second-order stationary point in O(max{εg⁻³ᐟ², εH⁻³}) second-order oracle evaluations.
  • Adding Lipschitz continuity of the third derivative yields a tensor-free accelerated method with Õ(max{εg⁻⁴ᐟ³, εH⁻²}) second-order evaluations.
  • An adaptive inexact Newton–CG variant avoids prior Lipschitz constants. Under bounded queried Hessians it has Õ(max{εg⁻⁵ᐟ³, εH⁻⁵ᐟ²}) Hessian-vector product complexity with high probability.
  • The improved rate still assumes higher-order smoothness. Oracle counts are distinct from total work, Õ hides logarithms, and preliminary experiments do not establish broad solver superiority.

Read first
Assumptions 2.1 and 2.3, the accelerated construction in Section 4 and Newton–CG analysis in Section 5.

3. Superlinear Convergence of Cyclic Gradient Methods with Historical Ritz Step Sizes

Ran Gu · arXiv:2610.32705 · Initial arXiv submission 23 October 2026 (UTC) · Primary record ↗ · Paper v1 ↗

Priority topics
Spectral gradient methods · superlinear convergence · numerical linear algebra

Why it matters
Analyzes actual delayed iterations rather than relying on a simplified spectral model.

Main result claims

  • For strictly convex quadratics with q=m+1 active distinct eigenvalues, the rolling cyclic Ritz rule in the existing LMSDR family has almost-everywhere doubly exponential convergence outside explicit exceptional initial weights.
  • Open convergence basins also exist for m+1<q≤2m with repetition count ℓ≥4. The sampled-history variant broadens the sufficient spectral regime under uniform sampling bounds and (2ℓ−1)ᵐ>binomial(q−1,m).
  • Sampling updates are included in the cost count. The results concern exact arithmetic and specified spectral regimes; they neither prove general nonlinear superlinearity nor settle almost-everywhere three-dimensional BB1 superlinearity.

Read first
Section 1.3 and Table 1 for scope, Theorem 3.2 for the delayed recurrence, and Section 5 for sampled history.

4. Splitting Compositions of Monotone Operators

Haoming Qu, Yefan Wu and Thomas Chaffey · arXiv:2610.39480 · Initial arXiv submission 29 October 2026 (UTC) · Primary record ↗ · Paper v1 ↗

Priority topics
Monotone operator equations · splitting · global linear convergence

Why it matters
Handles compositions of monotone factors whose composition may fail to be monotone.

Main result claims

  • For 0∈Hx+A(Bx)−b, introducing y∈Bx leads to triangular warped-resolvent iterations using one H evaluation and ordinary resolvents of A and B.
  • Theorem 4.1 certifies global linear convergence and residual error estimates when H is Lipschitz and the explicit two-block gain matrix has spectral radius below one. Sharpness is demonstrated on a linear family.
  • For pure compositions, one strongly monotone factor can suffice under the solution-existence and topology conditions of Theorem 5.3. This is weaker than joint contractivity and does not automatically yield the same linear rate for both components.
  • Circuit and equilibrium-network examples illustrate the method. Accessible resolvents and the gain condition remain practical restrictions; maximal monotonicity alone does not imply general convergence.

Read first
Algorithm 3.1 and Theorem 4.1; compare Theorems 5.1 and 5.3 before interpreting the one-factor result.
