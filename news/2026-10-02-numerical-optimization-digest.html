Numerical Optimization Digest · Issue 8

Proximal spectral gradients, geometric search, and stable learning updates

Published 2 October 2026 · Curated by Hassan Mohammad

Coverage: 25 September–1 October 2026, using primary-source publication/posting dates. One verified publisher paper and four independently selected arXiv papers. Google Scholar alerts received during the week were screened and deduplicated.

Peer-reviewed

Up to seven papers from Springer Nature, Elsevier, Taylor & Francis, SIAM and Wiley may be selected. This issue includes one paper with verified publication date and accessible primary-source claims.

1. Convergent simulated annealing generalized Barzilai-Borwein gradient method for nonsmooth optimization

Jiawei Xu, Bo He, Chengcheng Yan, Xu Zhang and Zheng Peng · Numerical Algorithms · Springer Nature · First online/posted 25 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Gradient methods · nonconvex optimization · global convergence · proximal methods

Why it matters
A practical combination of spectral stepsizes and proximal updates for composite objectives, with an annealing strategy to influence acceptance.

Main result claims

  • Method: Combines proximal gradients, Barzilai–Borwein stepsizes and simulated annealing for a smooth, possibly nonconvex term plus a convex nonsmooth term.
  • Theory: The authors establish global convergence under appropriate conditions and analyze iteration complexity for reaching an epsilon-stationary point.
  • Evidence: Numerical experiments are reported; the accessible abstract does not specify benchmark-level results.
  • Qualification: Full text is subscription restricted. No exact complexity exponent or detailed assumptions are asserted here.

Read first
Algorithm 1, then the convergence assumptions and epsilon-stationarity complexity result in the full paper.

arXiv

Four papers selected separately from the publisher ceiling. Conference acceptance reported on arXiv is stated explicitly; these entries are categorized by their arXiv source.

1. Retraction-Based Gradient Projection Algorithms on Manifolds

Conglong Xu and Hao Wu · arXiv:2609.30885 · First online/posted 25 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Gradient methods · convergence · numerical linear algebra

Why it matters
Extends projected-gradient reasoning to a retraction-specific notion of convexity, with applications to low-rank computation.

Main result claims

  • Theory: Defines retraction-specific convex sets and establishes convergence for gradient projection with several stepsize rules.
  • Application: Uses the framework for weighted low-rank approximation.
  • Evidence: Image-completion experiments provide numerical validation of the convergence analysis.
  • Qualification: The guarantees use the paper’s retraction-based convexity framework; they do not establish convergence for arbitrary nonconvex manifold problems.

Read first
The definitions of retraction-specific convexity, convergence results for each stepsize rule, and weighted low-rank examples.

2. Bayesian Optimization with Fisher Information Geometry: Gradient Bounds and Trust-Region Methods

Saksham Kiroriwal, Julius Pfrommer and Jürgen Beyerer · arXiv:2609.31107 · Authors report acceptance at NeurIPS 2026 · First online/posted 25 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Trust-region methods · gradient analysis · optimization in machine learning

Why it matters
Connects local surrogate sensitivity to acquisition-function gradients, suggesting a principled scaling rule for high-dimensional search.

Main result claims

  • Theory: Pulling back the Fisher metric through the posterior map gives a sensitivity tensor and an upper bound for gradients of reparameterizable acquisition functions.
  • Method: FITR replaces kernel-lengthscale scaling with local pullback-Fisher weights.
  • Evidence: Competitive performance is reported on squared-exponential GP benchmarks; gains with non-isotropic surrogates depend on the task.
  • Qualification: An acquisition-gradient bound is not a general global-convergence guarantee for Bayesian optimization.

Read first
The pullback-metric gradient bound, FITR construction, and the comparison across surrogate families.

3. Gradient Surgery for Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Thomas Borsani and Giuseppe Di Fatta · arXiv:2609.30966 · Authors report acceptance at ACML 2026 · First online/posted 25 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Gradient methods · nonconvex optimization · scientific computing

Why it matters
Addresses interference among loss components in PDE-informed training, a useful bridge between optimization methodology and scientific machine learning.

Main result claims

  • Observation: Across four benchmark problems, the authors identify phases with alternating angle-based and magnitude-based gradient conflict.
  • Method: PAM-GS adapts gradient surgery to the observed type of task interference.
  • Evidence: Four PDE benchmarks show competitive solution accuracy and strong task-balanced performance, outperforming alternatives on most problems.
  • Qualification: These are benchmark findings; the abstract supplies no general convergence theorem or universal accuracy guarantee.

Read first
The conflict-phase analysis, PAM-GS update rule, and per-problem comparisons of solution error and task balance.

4. Q-MINO: A Minimal-Norm Method for Quantization-Aware Training

Don Li · arXiv:2610.00738 · First online/posted 30 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · gradient methods · convergence analysis · optimization in machine learning

Why it matters
Uses recent optimization states to stabilize surrogate-gradient updates when low-bit quantization causes oscillations.

Main result claims

  • Method: A temporal bundle combines gradient consensus, state-drift regularization and an alignment constraint to produce minimum-norm directions.
  • Computation: A warm-started Frank–Wolfe procedure solves the constrained update subproblem, with a feasible fallback initialization.
  • Theory: The authors claim asymptotic neighborhood convergence through a stochastic Lyapunov–Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz framework.
  • Qualification: Neighborhood convergence does not establish convergence to an exact stationary point; the assumptions and quantization dependence require scrutiny.

Read first
The constrained direction subproblem, stochastic Lyapunov–KL assumptions, and experiments across quantization levels.
