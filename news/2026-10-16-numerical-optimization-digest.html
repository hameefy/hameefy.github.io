Numerical Optimization Digest · Issue 9

Nonconvex stationarity bounds, augmented Lagrangians, and stochastic gradients

Published 16 October 2026 · Curated by Hassan Mohammad

Weekly coverage: first-online publications and first-posted preprints from 9–15 October 2026 (Korea-local issue week). Two peer-reviewed papers and four arXiv-only preprints cleared the threshold. The archive has no issue for 2–8 October; that week is not silently folded into this issue.

Peer-reviewed publications

Two ranked method papers from the five named publisher groups, first published online during this week.

0. A proximal augmented Lagrangian method for nonconvex optimization with equality and inequality constraints

Adeyemi D. Adeoye, Puya Latafat and Alberto Bemporad · Computational Optimization and Applications · Springer Nature · First online 12 October 2026 · DOI 10.1007/s10589-026-00844-7 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · proximal methods · global convergence · nonlinear programming

Why it matters
The method handles smooth nonconvex objectives, a proximable convex nonsmooth term, and nonlinear equalities and inequalities, with an adaptive penalty and proximal schedule that addresses ill-conditioned inner solves.

Main result claims

  • Proved: Theorem 1 obtains an ε-KKT point in finitely many iterations for every ε above the limiting inner-solve tolerance, under the paper’s problem and parameter assumptions; the analysis relies on a feasible starting point.
  • Proved: Corollary 1 carries finite ε-KKT termination to the nonproximal augmented Lagrangian variant. The paper does not establish a worst-case iteration-complexity exponent.
  • Empirical: On 53 CUTEst QPs (44 convex, nine nonconvex), the authors compare with alpaqa using PANOC and proximal-gradient inner solvers, measuring success and gradient evaluations. In jointly successful PANOC runs alpaqa is cheaper on 35 of 48; with proximal gradients FS-P-ALM is cheaper on 26 of 42.
  • Qualification: A phase-I solve is needed when a feasible point is unavailable; the reported dense automatic-differentiation implementation limits the largest tested QPs.

Read first
Algorithm 1, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1; then Section 6.6 and Figures 3–4 for the CUTEst comparison.

1. On Distributionally Robust Multistage Convex Optimization: New Algorithms and Complexity Analysis

Shixuan Zhang and Xu Andy Sun · SIAM Journal on Optimization · SIAM · First online 15 October 2026 · DOI 10.1137/25M1761690 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Convex optimization · cutting-plane methods · complexity analysis · stochastic optimization

Why it matters
The paper changes which stages a decomposition algorithm visits, reducing the stated dependence on the number of stages in the subproblem count for distributionally robust multistage convex models.

Main result claims

  • Algorithm: The authors introduce nonconsecutive dual dynamic programming (NDDP), which adaptively explores stages rather than visiting them in the usual consecutive order.
  • Theory: Their complexity analysis reduces the number-of-stages dependence in subproblem complexity from quadratic to linear for the model and assumptions they study.
  • Empirical: The authors report two distributionally robust multistage examples to illustrate efficiency; the accessible publisher abstract does not give benchmark names, baselines, or numerical metrics, so no quantified performance advantage is asserted here.
  • Qualification: The guarantee concerns subproblem complexity for convex multistage models, not a general nonconvex or unconstrained solver.

Read first
The NDDP algorithm and its stage-complexity proof; then the two numerical examples in the full paper.

arXiv preprints

Four independently ranked preprints. Their claims are preliminary until peer review and replication.

0. Optimal Algorithms for Nonsmooth Nonconvex Stochastic Optimization with Constraints

Gon Buzaglo and Guy Kornowski · arXiv:2610.13787 · First posted 9 October 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · stochastic-gradient methods · oracle complexity · constrained optimization

Why it matters
It addresses whether convex constraints and nonsmoothness impose extra oracle cost when seeking the constrained Goldstein stationarity notion.

Main result claims

  • Upper bounds: The authors give projection-based and projection-free stochastic-gradient algorithms with Õ(δ−1ε−3) query complexity for a (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary point.
  • Zeroth-order bound: Their scalar-query algorithm achieves Õ(dδ−1ε−3) queries; the dimension dependence matches the lower bound in the stated oracle model.
  • Lower bound: Their Frank–Wolfe-gap construction, which also applies to smooth problems, establishes optimality of the stated stochastic-gradient rate.
  • Qualification: The result uses convex constraints and this specific approximate stationarity criterion; the tilde hides logarithmic factors. It is an arXiv preprint.

Read first
The projection and projection-free algorithm statements, the Frank–Wolfe-gap lower bound, and the reduction to online linear optimization.

1. On Global Joint Lower Bounds in Zeroth-Order Nonconvex Optimization

Yuxuan Xia and Xiao-Tong Yuan · arXiv:2610.19652 · First posted 14 October 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · zeroth-order methods · oracle complexity · machine-learning optimization

Why it matters
This complements the preceding preprint by testing whether the dimension, Goldstein radius, and stationarity tolerance are jointly unavoidable under a strong, fully adaptive scalar-query model.

Main result claims

  • Smooth lower bound: The authors prove Ω(dL02 min{ΔH,L02}/ε4) scalar queries under a globally lower-bounded smooth population objective and a second-moment condition on sample Lipschitz constants.
  • Reduction: A smooth construction yields a joint minimax lower bound in d, δ, and ε for Goldstein stationarity under explicit dimension and accuracy conditions.
  • Oracle strength: The lower bound allows adaptive full-space queries, sample-label selection, repeated use of a sampled function, and unrestricted output points; a separate exact-value lower bound covers randomized algorithms.
  • Qualification: The joint optimality statement applies in the authors’ specified high-dimensional and small-accuracy regimes; it is a lower-bound paper without an implementable solver.

Read first
The main lower-bound theorems, the smooth-to-Goldstein reduction, and the adaptive reusable-sample scalar-oracle construction.

2. On the convergence of doubly stochastic block gradient descent method for linear inverse problems

Yuxin Xia and Zehui Zhou · arXiv:2610.15399 · First posted 11 October 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Stochastic-gradient methods · convergence rates · numerical linear algebra · inverse problems

Why it matters
The update samples both one observation and one coordinate block, addressing separate data and signal-size bottlenecks in ill-posed linear inverse problems.

Main result claims

  • Proved: The authors establish regularization under a priori early stopping, with convergence rates when a source condition and a condition on the forward operator hold.
  • Stopping: They show finite discrepancy-principle stopping with high probability and monotone expected squared error before stopping under the stated assumptions.
  • Empirical: Section 5 uses Phillips, gravity, and Shaw Fredholm test problems; Tables 1–2 compare stopping index and reconstruction error against randomized block coordinate descent and Landweber-type variants.
  • Qualification: The theory concerns linear Hilbert-space inverse problems and regularized early stopping, not generic nonconvex optimization.

Read first
Theorems 2.1–2.3 and Section 4’s rate proof; then Section 5 and Tables 1–2.

3. A Rate Transfer Method: Convergence Rates in Stochastic Gradient Descent via Stochastic Calculus

Neil Walton · arXiv:2610.18739 · First posted 13 October 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Stochastic-gradient methods · convergence rates · optimization for machine learning

Why it matters
It provides a reusable route from a continuous-time Lyapunov inequality to a finite-time discrete stochastic-gradient bound.

Main result claims

  • Transfer theorem: Under moment, smoothness, and iterate-stability assumptions, Theorem 2 transfers the SDE Lyapunov estimate to SGD with an additional O(α(t)) forcing term inside the time integral.
  • Worked results: The analysis recovers classical strongly convex SGD rates and a geometric transient plus an O(h) neighborhood for stochastic heavy ball.
  • Qualification: The examples rederive known rates; a new optimizer still needs a suitable SDE, Lyapunov inequality, and bounded moments before this framework applies.

Read first
Theorem 2 in Section 3.2, then Sections 4.2–4.3 for SGD and stochastic heavy ball.
