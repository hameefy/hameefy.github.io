Weekly computational mathematics digest

Published 18 September 2026 · Curated by Hassan Mohammad

Coverage: first-online publications and first-posted preprints from 11–17 September 2026. No named-publisher article met the methodological threshold; seven arXiv preprints form this week’s independent research shortlist.

Peer-reviewed publications

No article first published online this week by Springer Nature, Elsevier, Taylor & Francis, SIAM, or Wiley cleared the digest’s methodological threshold. The indexed candidates were predominantly application-specific prediction, control, materials, biomedical, or engineering studies without a substantial new general optimization or nonlinear-solver contribution.

arXiv preprints

Seven substantive preprints, ranked independently of the publisher section.

1. Joint Structure Identification and Newton Acceleration via Proximal Line Search for Nonconvex Optimization

Yiming Zhou and Wei Dai · arXiv · First posted 16 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · proximal Newton methods · line search · signal processing

Why it matters
The method integrates active-set identification, inexact Newton acceleration, and globalization in one iteration. Its proximal line search is designed to preserve coordinates that reach nonsmooth kinks instead of undoing that structure during backtracking.

Main result claims

  • Theory: The authors prove finite backtracking and stationarity of accumulation points for their inexact proximal Newton framework.
  • Identification and rate: Under strict complementarity and a nonsingular reduced Hessian, they prove full-sequence convergence and finite active-set identification; a directional Dennis–Moré condition and vanishing relative inexactness yield eventual full steps and local Q-superlinear convergence.
  • Computation: The reported sparse-recovery and cost-sensitive kernel-SVM tests compare the proposed structure-preserving Newton scheme with first- and second-order alternatives and support the claimed benefit of retaining identified coordinates.
  • Qualification: The strongest local claims require strict complementarity, reduced-Hessian nonsingularity, and increasingly accurate model solves.

Read first
Algorithm 1 and Theorems 2–5, especially Section IV-A on finite identification and Section IV-B on Q-superlinear convergence; then Section V-C for the cost-sensitive kernel-SVM experiment.

2. The Complexity of Finding Stationary Points in Nonsmooth Nonconvex Optimization

Guy Kornowski · arXiv · First posted 15 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonconvex optimization · rates of convergence · oracle complexity

Why it matters
This closes a basic oracle-complexity question for nonsmooth nonconvex stationarity rather than proposing another upper-bound variant.

Main result claims

  • Goldstein stationarity: The author proves a worst-case lower bound of Ω(ΔL²/(δε³)) first-order queries for finding a (δ,ε)-Goldstein stationary point of an L-Lipschitz function with initial gap Δ.
  • Tightness: Theorem 3.1 matches existing upper bounds up to absolute constants, resolving the minimax query complexity in the stated oracle model.
  • Relaxed stationarity: Theorem 3.2 gives a tight Ω(ΔL²√λ/ε7/2) lower bound for the newer (λ,ε)-stationarity notion.
  • Qualification: These are worst-case information-theoretic results in a broad first-order oracle model; the paper contains no numerical experiments.

Read first
Table 1 and Theorems 3.1–3.2. Read Section 4 only if you need the resisting-oracle construction and dimension calibration.

3. An Adaptive Linesearch-free Method for Monotone Variational Inequalities under Local Lipschitz Continuity

Hongjia Ou, Andreas Themelis and Puya Latafat · arXiv · First posted 14 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Monotone operator equations · linesearch-free methods · adaptive splitting · logistic regression

Why it matters
The proposed adaptive forward-reflected-backward variants retain one resolvent and one new operator evaluation per iteration while avoiding both a global Lipschitz constant and a backtracking loop.

Main result claims

  • Sharp parameter range: The authors identify α>1/2 as the tight admissible range for constant reflection coefficients when the fixed step is sufficiently small.
  • Adaptivity and convergence: Algorithms 1–3 compute steps in closed form from local Lipschitz information; the analysis gives explicit positive lower bounds on step sizes and global convergence under local Lipschitz continuity.
  • Rate: A restricted dual-gap estimate decreases as O(1/Σγk), hence O(1/K) once generated step sizes are bounded below.
  • Experiments: Quadratic and constrained minimax, Cournot–Nash equilibrium, and sparse logistic-regression tests compare the adaptive variants with FRB, extragradient, FBF, GRAAL-type, and anchored-gradient baselines; results are illustrative rather than a universal performance claim.

Read first
Theorem 2.2 for the tight reflection range, Algorithms 1–3 and Theorem 3.4 for the Lyapunov/rate framework, then Figures 1–4—especially sparse logistic regression in Section 4.4.

4. Near-Optimal Exact-Value Zeroth-Order Complexity for Smooth Strongly Convex Optimization

Wendao Wu, Haihan Zhang, Chenheng Zhang, Yanyi Li, Chunyuan Zheng, Cong Fang, Haoxuan Li and Zhouchen Lin · arXiv · First posted 16 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Unconstrained optimization · zeroth-order methods · complexity · gradient approximation

Why it matters
The paper sharply characterizes what exact scalar function values can buy in smooth strongly convex optimization, including the unavoidable dimension factor in recovering directional information.

Main result claims

  • Upper bound: The authors combine finite differences, acceleration, and restart to obtain Nε ≤ Cd√κ[1+log+(Q/κ)], also taking the better accuracy-dominated branch, with Q=βR²/ε and κ=β/μ.
  • Lower bound: They prove Nε ≥ cd·min{√Q,√κ,(d/log(ed))1/3} using an exactly shielded smooth-chain construction and delayed rotations.
  • Near-optimal region: Theorem 3.7 and Corollary 3.8 match upper and lower bounds up to constants in the certified accuracy-dominated regime and at constant relative accuracy.
  • Qualification: The model assumes deterministic exact real-valued queries and free internal computation; arbitrary-precision and the remaining high-accuracy logarithmic gap are explicitly outside the resolved region.

Read first
Table 1, Theorems 3.4–3.7, and Corollary 3.8. Sections 8–12 contain the hard-instance compiler if you want to audit the lower bound.

5. Subspace methods for min-max problems

Morteza Kimiaei, Shima Shabani and Michael Breuß · arXiv · First posted 15 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Nonlinear equations · conjugate-gradient methods · line search · global convergence

Why it matters
This is a broad Jacobian-free framework for large monotone nonlinear equations arising from min–max problems, with both fixed-step and projected-line-search realizations and explicit residual complexity.

Main result claims

  • Framework: Four groups of Jacobian-free subspace directions, including classical and three-term conjugate-gradient updates, are stabilized through an angle condition and explicit scaling rule.
  • Global rate: Under monotonicity and Lipschitz continuity, the authors prove global convergence and a best-iterate residual rate O(ℓ−1/2), corresponding to O(ε−2) iterations.
  • Local rate: A local error bound gives o(ℓ−1/2) distance decay; a nonsingular Jacobian yields R-linear local convergence, geometric residual decay, and O(log ε−1) local complexity without strong monotonicity.
  • Evidence: The numerical study compares robust and efficient direction/step-size combinations on representative min–max problems; the 50-page framework is more valuable for its unification than any single winning variant.

Read first
Algorithm 1 and Proposition 2 for projected line search, then Sections 6.2–6.4—especially Theorem 3 and the local-linear result—and Section 7.3 for the focused comparison.

6. Global convergence of an efficient splitting method for the defocusing Gross–Pitaevskii ground state problem

Jiaxing Li, Shixin Zheng and Xiangxiong Zhang · arXiv · First posted 11 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Global convergence · splitting methods · nonlinear equations · GPU scientific computing

Why it matters
The paper proves convergence to the desired ground state for schemes whose expensive step is only a shifted-Laplacian solve, aligning rigorous globalization with GPU-friendly large-scale computation.

Main result claims

  • Global theorem: For monotone discrete Laplacians, Theorem 3.4 proves convergence from every positive normalized initial vector to the unique positive discrete ground state for any constant step below an explicit computable threshold.
  • Local rate: Theorem 5.1 establishes linear convergence once the iterates enter the ground-state neighborhood.
  • Cost structure: Each proposed Davis–Yin splitting step requires one inversion of a fixed shifted Laplacian, or a shifted Laplacian plus a separable potential, rather than an iterate-dependent elliptic operator.
  • Computation: Three-dimensional tests reach up to 999³ unknowns on one GPU and are reported as comparable in wall time to Riemannian conjugate-gradient methods while being more robust to initialization; the proof depends on positivity/monotonicity conditions on the discretization.

Read first
Table 1, the scheme and Lyapunov construction in Section 3.2, Theorem 3.4, Theorem 5.1, and the largest 3-D GPU experiment in Section 6.

7. A family of spectral conjugate gradient algorithms derived by least-squares approximations based on a modified quasi–Newton update with application to a revised robust binary classification model

Saman Babaie-Kafaki, Maryam Khoshsimaye-Bargard and Ahmad Mousavi · arXiv · First posted 11 September 2026 · Primary source ↗

Priority topics
Conjugate-gradient methods · quasi-Newton methods · global convergence · logistic regression and classification

Why it matters
The work links a modified secant equation, memoryless BFGS geometry, least-squares direction matching, and three-term spectral CG in a practical unconstrained solver.

Main result claims

  • Descent and convergence: Lemma 3.2 proves sufficient descent for MSTTMHS, and Theorem 3.5 establishes liminf ‖gk‖=0 under the stated bounded-level-set and line-search assumptions, without convexity.
  • Construction: The spectral parameter is obtained by least-squares alignment with a memoryless-BFGS direction derived from the modified secant equation, while preserving the Hestenes–Stiefel anti-jamming behavior.
  • Benchmarks: CUTEr tests compare four CG variants using Dolan–Moré profiles for iterations/function-gradient evaluations and runtime; the authors report an overall advantage for MSTTMHS.
  • Classification: On eight standard datasets, the revised robust quadratic-surface SVM is compared with four least-squares twin-SVM models for accuracy and CPU time; the evidence supports faster training with broadly comparable accuracy, not universal dominance.

Read first
Algorithm 3.1, Lemma 3.2 and Theorem 3.5; then Figure 1 for CUTEr performance profiles and Tables 1–2 for the eight-dataset classification comparison.
